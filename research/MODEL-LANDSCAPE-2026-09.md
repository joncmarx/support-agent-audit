# Calibration, not rank, should pick the judges

As of 30 September 2026, the draft lineup in Test Plan v2 mostly holds up, but the public evidence calls for four real changes and a handful of settings fixes. **First, drop DeepSeek V4 Pro** from the reviewer and Chinese open-weight trials. It now scores 36 on the main independent index, behind eight other Chinese open models, and it guesses on roughly nine in ten questions it cannot answer. Make **Z.ai's GLM-5.3 the reviewer front-runner** and add **Xiaomi's MiMo-V2.6-Pro**, which is now the top-scoring open-weight model. **Second, add Meta's Muse Spark 1.3 to the grader-2 trial.** It beats both Gemini 3.8 Flash and Qwen3.8 Max on the main capability index, ties the better of them on human preference, and comes close to Qwen's calibration at under a third of Qwen's cost per task. **Third, build the Western open-weight pipeline from Thinking Machines' Inkling (drafter) and NVIDIA's Nemotron 3 Ultra (reviewer).** Drop gpt-oss-120b and Muse Glimmer, rule out Mistral Medium 3.5 (its license bars any company with more than $20 million in monthly revenue), and write down whether Nemotron's Chinese-model synthetic training data counts against it. **Fourth, GPT-6 Astra is OpenAI's top model but not the most capable model available.** Claude Opus 5.5 holds that position, and it can serve as a true ceiling check if only the non-Anthropic graders score it. Across every role, the trait that matters most is calibration: a well-calibrated model says "not supported" or "I don't know" instead of guessing. Calibration tracks neither price, nor size, nor leaderboard rank. The evidence contradicts two of the audit's working assumptions: that bigger models make better reviewers, and that general leaderboards predict grounding and judging. It supports the cross-company grading rule mainly as insurance behind blind grading, and it is mixed on the other four. No public benchmark ranks any September 2026 model as a judge or reviewer, so the audit's own practice trials have to make the final picks.

## Two kinds of hallucination explain round one's bluffs

This report leans on a few terms. An **LLM-as-judge** is a language model used to grade another model's output against a rubric, which is the job of the audit's graders and labelers. An **open-weight** model is one whose trained parameters are published, so a company can run it on its own servers. **Thinking** (or reasoning effort) is hidden working-out text that a model writes before its answer. It is billed as output, and on many current models it cannot be switched off. **Quantization** stores a model's numbers at lower precision (fp8 is 8-bit, fp4 is 4-bit) to cut serving cost. A model's **native** precision is the format its lab released.

The evidence comes from four independent yardsticks, one platform catalog and a set of vendor claims. They are labelled throughout as **[I]** independent, **[A]** independent data reproduced by an aggregator, or **[V]** vendor-reported.

| Yardstick | What it measures | Snapshot | Label |
|---|---|---|---|
| Artificial Analysis (AA) Intelligence Index v4.3 | A composite of 10 tests weighted toward agentic work, science, knowledge and long-document reading | Fetched 30 Sep 2026; page shows no update date | [I] ([AA](https://artificialanalysis.ai/leaderboards/models)) |
| Arena text leaderboard | Human votes between paired answers, converted to an Elo-style score | 25 Sep 2026 | [I] ([Arena](https://arena.ai/leaderboard/text)) |
| AA-Omniscience hallucination rate | Closed-book guessing: of the questions a model did not get right, the share where it gave a wrong answer instead of declining | Fetched 30 Sep 2026; some values via BenchLM, 29 Sep | [I] ([AA](https://artificialanalysis.ai/evaluations/omniscience)) / [A] ([BenchLM](https://benchlm.ai/benchmarks/omnisciencehallucinationrate)) |
| Vectara HHEM-2.3 leaderboard | Document faithfulness: the share of summaries of a supplied document that add something the document does not support | Updated 22 Sep 2026 | [I] ([Vectara](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/vectara/hallucination-leaderboard/main/README.md)) |
| OpenRouter catalog | Prices, thinking settings, hosts | 30 Sep 2026 | Platform data ([OpenRouter](https://openrouter.ai/api/v1/models)) |
| Model cards | Each lab's own benchmark tables | Various | [V] |

**Elo** is the chess rating system. On Arena, a 100-point gap means voters prefer the higher-rated model about 64% of the time, and a 21-point gap means about 53% (standard Elo arithmetic).

The two hallucination measures often disagree, and the gap between them explains round one. **The audit's low-cost drafter, gpt-5.4-mini, is among the most faithful models of interest when it has a document (5.5% on Vectara) and among the worst guessers when it does not (a 90% closed-book hallucination rate)** ([Vectara](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/vectara/hallucination-leaderboard/main/README.md); [AA](https://artificialanalysis.ai/leaderboards/models)). Round one showed the same pattern. The bot rarely invented content when the docs covered a question, but 15 of v3's 18 bluffs landed in the "fix not in the docs" group (Test Plan v2, finding 4). The reviewer's real job, then, is to notice when a draft answers a question the docs do not cover, and that job is a test of calibration. Grok shows the reverse profile: Grok 4.7 guesses on only 29% of closed-book misses, but its recent "fast" predecessors were the least faithful models of interest on Vectara (18–20%) ([Vectara](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/vectara/hallucination-leaderboard/main/README.md)).

Calibration does not follow price. Alibaba's Qwen3.8 Max guesses on 29% of misses, while Anthropic's $10/$50 Claude Fable 5.1 guesses on 73% and Claude Opus 5.5 on 59% at maximum effort ([AA](https://artificialanalysis.ai/leaderboards/models)). One caution applies to Vectara: its most faithful models also write the shortest summaries (gpt-5.4-mini averages 55 words, GPT-6 Astra 149), so part of their lead may come from making fewer claims rather than more careful ones ([Vectara](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/vectara/hallucination-leaderboard/main/README.md)).

### The measuring stick: labelers, graders and the tie-breaker

**Labeler 1 should be Claude Opus 5.5 on the Max plan.** It leads the AA index (57.6) and ties Fable 5.1 for the best long-document reading score in the set (0.85). Its knowledge accuracy (0.66) helps decide whether a fix is "general technical knowledge" ([AA](https://artificialanalysis.ai/leaderboards/models)). Its weakness is that it guesses on 59% of closed-book misses at maximum effort. The plan's second labeler from another company and JC's review of disagreements are the right counterweights.

**For grader 1, compare the two Claude models at matched cost rather than matched effort.** The plan asks whether Opus is "clearly better" enough to justify doubling the cost. Per token, Opus does cost double. Per task on AA, however, Opus at medium effort scores 51.2 for $1.34, while Sonnet at high effort scores 46.7 for $1.08. Opus at low effort (42.3, $0.55) matches Sonnet at medium (40.7, $0.59) for the same money ([AA](https://artificialanalysis.ai/leaderboards/models)). Sonnet is also unusually sensitive to effort: it drops 15 points from maximum to medium, where Opus drops 6. The plan's default of "medium for every role" would therefore handicap Sonnet. Sonnet's edge is calibration (a 47% guessing rate against Opus's 59%). Neither model appears on any judge benchmark, and Sonnet is not yet rated on Arena.

**For grader 2, Muse Spark 1.3 is the front-runner on public evidence, but it should enter the trial rather than replace it.** It scores 48.1 on AA, above Qwen3.8 Max (45.4) and Gemini 3.8 Flash (40.9). It ranks #9 on Arena, next to Gemini at #10 and well above Qwen at #25. It guesses on 33% of misses, close to Qwen's 29% and far below Gemini's 55%. It reads long documents well (0.83), streams at 184 tokens per second, and costs $1.60 per AA task ([AA](https://artificialanalysis.ai/leaderboards/models); [Arena](https://arena.ai/leaderboard/text)). Meta sells it through a paid API ([Meta](https://developer.meta.com/ai/models/muse-spark/)). Gemini 3.8 Flash remains the cheapest and fastest option, but launch coverage notes that its knowledge stops at January 2025 in some domains ([9to5Google](https://9to5google.com/2026/09/02/gemini-3-8-flash-launch/)), and that matters for a labeler judging 2026 fixes. Qwen3.8 Max is the best calibrated of the three, but it is costly and slow: **$5.41 per AA task (4.4 times Gemini's), 39 tokens per second, and a default effort of "xhigh" that silently maximizes spend** unless it is set explicitly ([AA](https://artificialanalysis.ai/leaderboards/models); [OpenRouter](https://openrouter.ai/api/v1/models)). The plan's budget assumed grader 2 costs under 1¢ per reply, so a Qwen win would raise grading costs. The runner-up becomes the tie-breaker, as the plan intends.

### The bot under test: drafters, reviewer and ceiling

**Keep gpt-5.4-mini as the low-cost drafter, and expect its known profile:** faithful to the docs but a guesser beyond them. Pin its thinking to whatever round one used, because on this model thinking can be switched off entirely ([OpenRouter](https://openrouter.ai/api/v1/models)).

**The independence rules cap the arm-B reviewer at "near-frontier," and the case study should say so.** On AA, the two frontier labs are Anthropic and OpenAI, and the rules exclude both from reviewing. The strongest allowed candidates score between 44 and 48, about 10 points below the top ([AA](https://artificialanalysis.ai/leaderboards/models)). Arm B therefore tests "a stronger independent reviewer," not "a frontier reviewer."

Within that band, **GLM-5.3 is the best-balanced reviewer candidate.** It guesses on 30% of misses, the lowest of the three draft candidates. It scores 45 on AA and finishes a task end-to-end in 38.6 seconds, against 65–69 seconds for MiMo and Kimi. It spent 14.8 million thinking tokens on AA's knowledge test, against Kimi K3's 52.7 million ([AA](https://artificialanalysis.ai/leaderboards/models); [AA-Omniscience](https://artificialanalysis.ai/evaluations/omniscience)). **MiMo-V2.6-Pro** scores highest (46.3) and is by far the cheapest at $0.13 per AA task. It is MIT-licensed, but it guesses more (41%), writes very long final answers, and was released only on 21 September ([AA open-weights](https://artificialanalysis.ai/models/open-source); [HF](https://huggingface.co/XiaomiMiMo/MiMo-V2.6-Pro-RL)). **Kimi K3** knows the most of any Chinese open model and leads them on Arena (1488). It also guesses on 53% of misses, costs $3 / $15, and is the heaviest thinker in the group. Heavy thinking is the same habit that ran round one's trial reviewer out of room in 24 of 55 calls (Test Plan v2, finding 9).

The **GLM-5.3-Flash** alternate is the best-calibrated Chinese open model (28%) at $0.25 per AA task, and it is MIT-licensed ([HF](https://huggingface.co/zai-org/GLM-5.3-Flash)). One practical caution applies to both GLM models: Zhipu (Z.ai) has been on the US Entity List since January 2025 ([Sheppard Mullin via NLR](https://natlawreview.com/article/choosing-between-us-and-chinese-ai-models-export-control-risks-both-sides)). A portfolio aimed at US hiring managers loses nothing by pinning a third-party fp8 host, such as Parasail, instead of Z.ai's own endpoint.

**GPT-6.1 Sol is the right premium drafter, with one caveat.** It scores 50.2 on AA at high effort for $0.32 per task, making it the most token-efficient frontier model, and its long-document reading score is 0.83 ([AA](https://artificialanalysis.ai/leaderboards/models)). The caveat is age: it was listed on 29 September, so it has no Arena rating and no faithfulness score yet ([OpenRouter](https://openrouter.ai/api/v1/models)). **GPT-6 Sol** costs the same, lets thinking be switched off, and has a directly measured 6.5% on Vectara. Against that, it scores lower on AA (47.5) and guesses on 60% of closed-book misses. The "-pro" versions of both Sols are the same model run with far more thinking, and they should be avoided.

**GPT-6 Astra answers "would OpenAI's best do better?", not "would the most sophisticated model do better?"** Astra beats GPT-6.1 Sol by only 0.9 AA points at 4.5 times the cost per task, and it ranks #26 on Arena, below Gemini 3.8 Flash ([AA](https://artificialanalysis.ai/leaderboards/models); [Arena](https://arena.ai/leaderboard/text)). Claude Opus 5.5 leads both leaderboards. If JC wants the literal answer, run Opus on the 43 practice questions as well, graded only by grader 2 and the tie-breaker so that no Anthropic model grades Anthropic text. The comparison has a built-in twist: the most capable model is also the most willing of the top models to guess, which is exactly the trust incident the scoreboard counts.

### Two open-weight pipelines, one clear capability gap

**E-CN should reuse the arm-B reviewer and trial MiMo-V2.6-Pro against Kimi K3 as the drafter.** Reusing the reviewer means E-CN and arm B differ only in who drafts, which makes the comparison cleaner. MiMo leads on AA, costs about a seventh of Kimi's input price and a seventeenth of its output price, and carries the simplest license (MIT). Kimi's custom license adds conditions for large model-hosting businesses ([HF Kimi K3 license](https://huggingface.co/moonshotai/Kimi-K3/blob/main/LICENSE)). Moonshot is also named in reported White House options to "effectively ban" specific Chinese models ([Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/91576757/trumps-proposed-ban-on-chinese-ai-models-could-strengthen-beijings-hand)), and a company that self-hosts would count that as switch risk. On the question the arm asks, no US law bars a private company from self-hosting these models. What has been enacted is narrower: a Defense Department ban that reaches DoD contractors and names DeepSeek ([ETO AGORA](https://agora.eto.tech/instrument/2694)). Congress has sent letters to Airbnb, Anysphere and DoorDash asking about their use of Chinese models ([Industrial Cyber](https://industrialcyber.co/critical-infrastructure/us-congress-deepens-investigation-into-chinese-ai-models-over-critical-infrastructure-and-data-security-risks/)), which supports the plan's framing.

**E-US should pair Inkling (drafter) with Nemotron 3 Ultra (reviewer), and the case study should expect a capability gap without a matching trust gap.** Western open weights trail badly on capability. The best, Inkling Small, scores 26 on AA, against 46 for the best Chinese open model. On Arena, the best Western open model (Gemma 4 31B) sits 35 points below Kimi K3 ([AA](https://artificialanalysis.ai/leaderboards/models); [Arena](https://arena.ai/leaderboard/text)). On calibration, however, **Nemotron 3 Ultra guesses on 30% of misses [A], level with GLM-5.3** ([BenchLM](https://benchlm.ai/benchmarks/omnisciencehallucinationrate)). That independent figure backs NVIDIA's own claim of high abstention [V], and NVIDIA lists "high-stakes RAG" (answering from retrieved documents, which is this bot's job) as a best use ([HF card](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/NVIDIA-Nemotron-3-Ultra-550B-A55B-BF16)). Inkling ranks second among Western open models on both AA (25) and Arena (1442). It is Apache-licensed, and its own card lists hallucination and imprecise instruction-following as limitations [V] ([HF card](https://huggingface.co/thinkingmachines/Inkling)).

Four candidates should leave the E-US trial. **gpt-oss-120b** scores 12 on AA, ranks #208 on Arena and is 13 months old. **Muse Glimmer** scores 17, and keeping Meta out of the arms preserves Muse Spark's eligibility as a grader. **Mistral Medium 3.5** has a license that bars companies with more than $20 million in monthly revenue, which excludes the very companies this arm speaks to, and it guesses on 82% of misses ([license](https://huggingface.co/mistralai/Mistral-Medium-3.5-128B/blob/main/LICENSE); [AA](https://artificialanalysis.ai/leaderboards/models)). **PrismML's Ternary Bonsai 2 27B** is derived from Qwen3.8-27B, so it counts as Chinese under the plan's own lineage rule ([HF card](https://huggingface.co/prism-ml/Ternary-Bonsai-2-27B-gguf)).

The Nemotron lineage question needs a written rule. Its weights are NVIDIA's own, but its card discloses pretraining data generated by DeepSeek-V3/R1 and Qwen models ([HF card](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/NVIDIA-Nemotron-3-Ultra-550B-A55B-BF16)). Other Western labs do not disclose which models generated their synthetic data, so a strict "no Chinese-derived data" rule penalizes the one vendor that is transparent. The defensible wording is "no Chinese-origin weights," stated in the case study, with the data caveat noted.

| (d) The grader must come from a different company | **Supported, as insurance** | Judges favor familiar, low-perplexity text whoever wrote it ([Wataoka et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.21819)). Blind grading removes most self-preference, while authorship labels inflate it ([Chae et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18091)) | Keep the rule; blind grading does most of the work. The reviewer–grader extension is untested |
| (e) Hosted "open-weight" results represent self-hosting | **Mixed** | The choice of backend alone shifted benchmark scores by up to 16.6 points ([GreaterWrong](https://www.greaterwrong.com/posts/KsyoSAyBRXtwzSugg/not-pinning-your-openrouter-provider-might-invalidate-your)). fp8 was effectively lossless in a large 2024 study ([Kurtic et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.02355)), and Kimi K3 hosts that pass Moonshot's verification match the official API ([KVV](https://github.com/MoonshotAI/Kimi-Vendor-Verifier)) | Valid only for a pinned, native-precision, verified host, which represents a well-run deployment rather than a typical one |
| (f) Higher reasoning effort improves judging and reduces hallucination | **Mixed; contradicted for faithfulness** | On Vectara, the same model is slightly less faithful at higher effort (gpt-5.2: 8.4% low vs 10.8% high; DeepSeek V3 6.1% vs R1 11.3%) ([Vectara](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/vectara/hallucination-leaderboard/main/README.md)). Three of four flagships guess more at maximum than at high effort. Explicit reasoning helps judges on hard pairs ([Soumik](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.23178)) | Use medium or high effort for judges and low or medium for drafters, and measure the effect |
| (g) General "intelligence" leaderboards predict grounding and judging | **Contradicted** | gpt-5.4-mini scores 24 on AA yet is among the most faithful models on Vectara. Judge rankings reshuffle across benchmarks: 11 of 21 judges moved four or more places ([Norman et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.19544v1)). Kimi K3's index score rose while its guessing rose ([Kili](https://kili-technology.com/blog/kimi-k3s-benchmarks-and-hallucinations----what-that-tells-us-about-ai-evaluation)) | Use leaderboards to shortlist and practice trials to decide |

Assumptions (a) and (b) cannot both hold on the same yardstick. On AA, Grok 4.7 (46.4) ties the best Chinese open model (46.3). On Arena, Gemini 3.8 Flash (1492) beats every Chinese open model. If Chinese open models count as "close to the frontier," then so do xAI on one measure and Google on the other. The exclusions still stand, but for role-specific reasons: Grok's lineage has weak document faithfulness, and Google competes for a grading slot instead.

Assumption (d) has a gap the audit should acknowledge. Company independence stands in for independence of judgment, and distillation can blur it. Anthropic has accused Moonshot of training on 3.4 million Claude exchanges ([Tom's Hardware](https://www.tomshardware.com/tech-industry/artificial-intelligence/moonshot-releases-2-8-trillion-parameter-kimi-k3)), and a CNBC headline extends the accusation to DeepSeek and Alibaba ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/11/chinese-ai-labs-moonshot-deepseek-alibaba-anthropic.html)). These are allegations, not findings. If they are true, however, a Qwen grader-2 or a Kimi reviewer could share some of Claude's taste, which is the correlation the rules exist to prevent. That is a quiet argument for Muse Spark or Gemini as grader 2 and for GLM or MiMo as reviewer.

On (f), the closed-book evidence cuts both ways by model. GPT-6 Astra, GPT-6.1 Sol and Fable 5.1 all guess more at maximum effort than at high, while Opus 5.5 guesses less ([AA](https://artificialanalysis.ai/leaderboards/models)). Maximum effort also delays the first word of a reply by 4 to 11 minutes on the top models, which rules it out for routine grading.

## No public benchmark can pick a judge for this audit

The largest blind spot is judging itself. **No public judge benchmark covers any September 2026 model.** JudgeBench's leaderboard was last modified on 31 October 2025, and RewardBench 2 tops out at Claude Opus 4 and Gemini 2.5 ([JudgeBench](https://huggingface.co/api/spaces/ScalerLab/JudgeBench); [RewardBench 2](https://awesomepapers.io/benchmarks/rewardbench-2)). The freshest systematic study, covering 21 judges up to GPT-5.4 and Claude Opus 4.6, found that on MT-Bench, raw agreement overstates chance-corrected agreement by 34 to 41 percentage points. It recommends Cohen's κ, an agreement score that subtracts what two graders would match on by luck ([Norman et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.19544v1)). The audit should report κ, not percent agreement.

Document faithfulness is almost as dark. Vectara has no entry for any Claude 5.x model, Gemini 3.8 Flash, GPT-6.1 Sol, Grok 4.7, Qwen3.8, GLM-5.3, Kimi K3, MiMo, Nemotron 3 Ultra or Inkling ([Vectara](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/vectara/hallucination-leaderboard/main/README.md)). Nothing public measures the reviewer's actual task, which is spotting unsupported claims in someone else's draft. Nothing measures over-blocking either, and over-blocking was round one's real reviewer failure (87 of 97 drafts blocked). Public data measures whether a model guesses. It does not measure whether a cautious model flags too much, so well-calibrated reviewers such as GLM-5.3 could over-block. Arm B's prediction, which counts both correct drafts let through and bluffs caught, is the right test.

Four smaller gaps also fall to the practice trials. The sources do not document whether thinking can be switched off for GLM-5.3, Kimi K3, MiMo, Nemotron or Inkling. Calibration and instruction-following numbers for Western open weights are mostly vendor-reported [V]. No 2026 study isolates fp4 against fp8 quality for these specific models. And no study tests whether a distilled model shares its teacher's grading taste.

The trials have limited resolution of their own. JC's 20 blind grades anchor the grader choice. If a candidate agrees with him on 16 of 20, the plausible true range runs from about 58% to 92% (a standard 95% interval, our arithmetic). Two graders would need to differ by roughly 20 points or more before 20 grades could separate them. The grader trial therefore needs a tie rule written in advance, in the same spirit as the arm-winner rule in section 6 of the test plan. A sensible version: if κ ranges overlap, prefer the better-calibrated, then the cheaper candidate. The same rule should also require rendering every reply as plain text before grading, because a 2026 study of five judge models found that they preferred markdown-formatted answers 73–97% of the time, against 57% for humans ([Soumik](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.23178)).

## Conclusion

The public evidence reframes the audit's central choice. JC's instinct was to buy judgment by buying a frontier model. The data says the judgment a support bot needs most is knowing when the docs do not cover a question, and that skill is nearly independent of price, size and rank. The low-cost drafter's public profile (faithful but a guesser) forecast round one's bluffs before any test ran. That makes calibration, not general intelligence, the property to pre-register and measure for every reviewer and grader. It also means arm B can succeed or fail for reasons that have nothing to do with being "frontier." The rules already cap that reviewer about 10 points below the top.

For the case study, the most useful new prediction concerns E-US. Western open weights sit about 20 AA points behind Chinese open weights, but Nemotron 3 Ultra guesses no more often than GLM-5.3. A support leader bound by a no-Chinese-models policy may therefore give up resolution rate without giving up trust. The plan already predicts E-US resolves at least 3 fewer questions than E-CN. Adding a second pre-registered prediction, that E-US stays within the same trust budget as E-CN, turns the procurement question into the sharper answer a support leader can act on.
